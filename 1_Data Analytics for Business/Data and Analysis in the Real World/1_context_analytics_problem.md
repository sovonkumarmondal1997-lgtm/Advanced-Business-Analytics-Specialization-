# Topic: How to Think About Analytical Problems in Business Analytics

## Simplified Explanation:

This lesson explains that successful business analytics is not just about analyzing data—it is about making better business decisions. The best analysts are naturally curious people who enjoy understanding how things work and turning confusion into clarity. However, analysis only has value if it leads to meaningful action.

A key difference between business analytics and academic research is that businesses expect a return on investment (ROI). Simply gaining knowledge is not enough; the analysis must help improve decisions and create business value. Since businesses have limited time and money, analysts must focus on work that truly matters.

The Most Important Principle: Think Backwards

Instead of starting with data, start with the decision you need to make.

### Follow this process:

1. Identify the decision
- What business decision are you trying to make?
- Will different analytical results lead to different actions?

2. Define the desired output
- What would the final result look like?
- Would it be a chart, graph, report, or presentation?
- How would you recognize different outcomes in the data?

3. Plan the analysis
- What methods and tools are needed?
- Use the simplest approach that can answer the question.
- Complex methods should only be used when necessary because they are harder to explain and justify.
    
4. Determine the required data
- What data is needed?
- Does the data already exist?
- Has someone already performed a similar analysis?
- Can the data be obtained from a data warehouse, business intelligence system, external source, or experiment?

5. Check feasibility
- Can the analysis be completed within the required timeframe?
- Is the effort worth the business value?
- If the answer is needed by Friday but the analysis takes two weeks, you need a different approach.

Only after answering these questions should you begin collecting data and performing the analysis.


# Topic: Building a Conceptual Business Model for Analytics

## Simplified Explanation:

In the previous lesson, we learned to think backwards from a business decision to the information needed to make that decision. This lesson introduces a tool called a Conceptual Business Model, which helps analysts understand the business context behind the data.

### What is a Conceptual Business Model?

A conceptual business model is a diagram that shows:

1. The important elements of a business.
2. How those elements are connected.
3. The relationships and interactions that generate data.

Its purpose is not just to understand data, but to understand how the business actually works and what questions are worth analyzing.

### Example: In-Flight Internet Service

The instructor uses an airline Wi-Fi business as an example.

1. The basic elements are:

- Customer – the person using the service.
- Flight – where the service is used.
- Purchase – when the customer pays for the service.
- Session – the actual use of the internet service.

2. These elements are connected. For example:

- One flight can have many customers.
- One customer can take many flights.
- One customer can make many purchases and have many sessions over time.

These are called one-to-many or many-to-many relationships. Mapping these relationships helps analysts understand how different business events influence one another.

### Expanding the Model

As understanding grows, more business elements can be added:

- Devices (laptop, tablet, phone)
- Applications or web browsers
- Internet products (30-minute pass, full-flight pass, subscription)
- Sales channels (online, airport, onboard)
- Promotions and discounts
- Aircraft type
- Airline
- Flight details (route, duration, departure time)
- Customer demographics and preferences
- Communication channels
- Network performance metrics (speed, latency)
- Revenue and accounting systems

Each new element helps explain customer behavior and business performance.

### Why is This Useful for Analytics?

1. A conceptual business model helps analysts ask meaningful questions, such as:

- Which devices do customers use most?
- Do promotions increase purchases?
- Does internet usage vary by airline or aircraft type?
- Does flight delay affect customer behavior?

2. The model also helps analysts identify:

- What data is needed.
- Where that data comes from.
- How different data sources can be connected.
