# Topic: Roles and Responsibilities in a Data & Analytics Organization

## Simplified Explanation:

So far, the course has focused on how data moves through the analytics process—from generation and storage to analysis and business action. This lesson focuses on who performs these activities within an organization. Understanding these roles helps you collaborate effectively and navigate the data environment.

### Major Data & Analytics Functions

Organizations typically perform five major data-related functions:

- Data Architecture – Designing the overall data environment.
- Data Management – Building and maintaining databases and data systems.
- Reporting – Producing regular reports and dashboards.
- Ad-Hoc Analysis – Answering specific business questions through analysis.
- Modeling – Applying advanced statistical and predictive techniques.

The last three functions (reporting, analysis, and modeling) are often grouped under data mining, which means extracting useful insights from data.

### Technical IT-Focused Roles:

1. Infrastructure Team

Responsible for:

- Servers
- Networks
- Hardware
- System connectivity

They keep the technical environment running.

2. System & Application Developers

Responsible for:

- Building business applications
- Maintaining operational systems
- Supporting software environments

These systems often generate the data used for analytics.

### Data Management & BI Roles:

1. Data Architect

Designs:

- Database structures
- Data models
- Enterprise data environments

They decide how data should be organized.

2. Database Administrator (DBA)

Responsible for:

- Database performance
- Security
- Stability
- Maintenance

They also help optimize SQL queries when performance issues occur.

3. ETL Developer

Responsible for:

- Extracting data
- Transforming data
- Loading data into databases

They ensure data is correctly moved and integrated.

4. BI Developer

Responsible for:

- Business Intelligence tools
- Dashboard implementation
- Report automation
- Report distribution

They bridge the gap between IT and business reporting.

### Analytics-Focused Roles:

Database Analyst
Writes SQL queries.
Extracts data directly from databases.
Performs basic analysis.

Strong technical focus.

1. Data Analyst

- Analyzes data.
- Interprets results.
- Answers business questions.
- Connects data insights to business decisions.

This is the core analytics role in many organizations.

2. Modeler

A more advanced analyst who focuses on:

- Predictive analytics
- Prescriptive analytics
- Statistical modeling
- Advanced analytical techniques

Often works with machine learning and forecasting models.

3. Business Analyst

Focuses on:

- Business processes
- Operational improvements
- System enhancements

Unlike data analysts, their primary goal is improving how the business operates rather than analyzing data itself.

4. Data Scientist

The Data Scientist is often viewed as a combination of several roles.

A data scientist typically has:

- Technical skills (SQL, programming, data manipulation)
- Statistical and modeling expertise
- Business knowledge
- Communication skills
- Problem-solving ability

They can move across multiple areas of analytics and connect technical findings to business actions. Modern organizations increasingly value professionals who can blend these skills rather than specialize in only one area.

### Simple Career Hierarchy:

Infrastructure / Developers
           ↓
Data Architect
           ↓
DBA / ETL Developer / BI Developer
           ↓
Database Analyst
           ↓
Data Analyst
           ↓
Modeler
           ↓
Data Scientist

(Business Analysts often work alongside these roles rather than within the same hierarchy.)

Key Takeaway:

A successful analytics environment depends on many specialized roles, from infrastructure teams and database administrators to analysts and data scientists. The most valuable professionals are increasingly those who combine technical skills, analytical expertise, business understanding, and communication abilities, allowing them to turn data into meaningful business action.



# Topic: Organizational Structures for Analytics Teams

## Simplified Explanation:

This lesson explains how analytics teams are organized within companies. The structure of an analytics team can affect the quality of analysis, speed of decision-making, resource efficiency, and how well analysts understand the business.

### Why Organizational Structure Matters:

Analytics does not happen in isolation. Analysts work with different business functions such as:

- Marketing
- Finance
- Operations
- Customer Service

Organizations must decide:

Should all analysts work in one central team, or should they be spread across different business units?

This is the balance between centralization and decentralization.

### Four Common Analytics Organization Models:

1. Centralized Model

All analysts work in one central analytics team.

Advantages:

- Consistent methods and standards.
- Better alignment with company-wide priorities.
- Efficient use of resources.
- Strong knowledge sharing among analysts.

Disadvantages:

- Less responsive to individual departments.
- Analysts may have less business context.
- Departments have less control over priorities.

2. Allocated Model

Analysts belong to a central team, but specific analysts are assigned to support particular business functions.

Advantages:

- Better responsiveness to business units.
- Improved business knowledge and context.
- Maintains some consistency and governance.

Disadvantages:

- Difficult to balance resource allocation.
- May require more staff than a fully centralized model.

3. Coordinated Model

Analytics teams are embedded within business functions but remain connected through governance, standards, user groups, or centers of excellence.

Advantages:

- High responsiveness.
- Strong business context.
- Some consistency across teams.

Disadvantages:

- Coordination is difficult.
- Duplicate work may occur.
- Different teams may produce different answers to similar questions ("multiple versions of the truth").
- Requires more resources.

4. Distributed Model

Each business unit has its own independent analytics team with little or no coordination.

Advantages:

- Maximum responsiveness.
- Deep business expertise.
- Full flexibility in methods and tools.

Disadvantages:

- Little standardization.
- High duplication of effort.
- Highest staffing requirements.
- Increased risk of inconsistent results.

### Comparison of the Models:
Model	      Responsiveness	  Consistency	  Business Context	  Resource Efficiency
Centralized	       Low	             High	            Low	                 High
Allocated	    Medium-High	         High	         Medium-High	        Medium
Coordinated	       High	            Medium	            High	          Medium-Low
Distributed     Very High	         Low	          Very High	             Low

### Factors That Influence the Best Model:

1. Company Size

Large organizations can support multiple analytics teams.

Small organizations often benefit from centralization because they may not have enough analysts to create several effective teams.

2. Similarity of Analytical Methods

If departments use similar analytical techniques, centralization works well.

If departments require highly specialized methods, decentralization may be better.

3. Geographic Distribution

When teams are spread across countries and time zones, decentralized structures often become more practical.

4. Business Context

The closer analysts are to a business function, the deeper their understanding of that area becomes.

This is one of the strongest arguments for decentralized structures.

### Real-World Trend

Most organizations try to balance:

- Efficiency and consistency (centralized benefits)
- Responsiveness and business understanding (decentralized benefits)

Because of this, many companies adopt either:

- Allocated Model
- Coordinated Model

These often provide the best compromise between control and flexibility.

Key Takeaway:

There is no single "best" analytics organization structure. The choice depends on company size, business needs, analyst expertise, geography, and the importance of business context. In practice, many successful organizations use Allocated or Coordinated models because they balance efficiency, consistency, responsiveness, and business understanding.
