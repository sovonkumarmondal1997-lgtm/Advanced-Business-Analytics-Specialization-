# Introduction to Prescriptive Analytics

Prescriptive analytics is the third stage of customer analytics. While descriptive analytics explains what happened and predictive analytics forecasts what is likely to happen, prescriptive analytics focuses on recommending actions. Its goal is to determine what a company should do to achieve a desired business outcome. Instead of only understanding customer behavior or predicting future behavior, prescriptive analytics helps managers choose actions that influence and improve those outcomes. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## The Three Types of Analytics

### Descriptive Analytics
Descriptive analytics collects and summarizes data to identify patterns, trends, and relationships. It answers:

- What happened?
- What patterns exist in the data?

### Predictive Analytics
Predictive analytics uses historical behavior to forecast future outcomes. It answers:

- What is likely to happen next?
- How will customers behave in the future?

### Prescriptive Analytics
Prescriptive analytics goes one step further by recommending actions. It answers:

- What should the company do?
- Which action will produce the best outcome?

Its purpose is to influence future behavior rather than simply describe or predict it. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Components of a Prescriptive Analytics Problem

Every prescriptive analytics problem consists of three key elements:

### 1. Objective (Goal)

The objective is the outcome the company wants to maximize, minimize, or achieve.

Examples:

- Maximize sales.
- Maximize profit.
- Increase market share.
- Improve customer retention.
- Reduce costs.

The objective defines what success looks like.

### 2. Actions (Decision Variables)

Actions are the decisions a company can control to influence outcomes.

Examples:

- Changing product prices.
- Offering discounts.
- Increasing advertising spending.
- Launching promotions.
- Adjusting service levels.

These are the levers managers can use to influence customer behavior.

### 3. Model

A model connects actions to outcomes.

The model explains:

- How actions affect customer behavior.
- How changing a decision influences the objective.

Without a model, companies cannot reliably predict the consequences of their actions. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Optimization

Optimization is the process of finding the action that best achieves the objective.

In the pricing example:

- Objective = Maximize quantity sold.
- Action = Set the price.
- Model = Demand equation.

Using the model, the company can test different prices and identify the one that produces the highest sales volume.

The process works as follows:

1. Define the goal.
2. Identify controllable actions.
3. Use a model to predict outcomes.
4. Choose the action that produces the best outcome.

This is the essence of prescriptive analytics. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

## Important Limitation

Maximizing quantity sold does not always mean maximizing profit.

For example:

- Setting price to ₹0 may maximize quantity sold.
- However, revenue and profit could become zero or negative.

This illustrates an important principle:

The quality of a prescriptive recommendation depends on selecting the correct objective. Companies must carefully define what they truly want to optimize before making decisions. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

## Role of Prescriptive Analytics

Prescriptive analytics combines insights from descriptive and predictive analytics:

### Descriptive Analytics
Provides understanding of customer behavior.

### Predictive Analytics
Forecasts future outcomes.

### Prescriptive Analytics
Uses that knowledge to recommend actions that improve business performance.

It transforms analytics from observation and prediction into decision-making and optimization. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

## Key Takeaway

Prescriptive analytics focuses on determining the best action to achieve a business objective. Every prescriptive problem requires three elements: an objective (what the company wants to achieve), actions (what the company can control), and a model (how actions affect outcomes). By combining these elements and applying optimization, businesses can move beyond predicting customer behavior and begin making data-driven decisions that actively improve results.
