# Descriptive Analytics:

Descriptive Analytics is the process of systematically collecting, organizing, and interpreting data to support business decisions. Its main purpose is to provide actionable information that helps organizations understand customers, markets, and business performance. The foundation of descriptive analytics is good-quality data combined with decision-making needs.

Managers typically face three types of business questions:

### Exploratory Questions
These questions aim to discover why something is happening. For example, if a company's sales suddenly decline, managers may want to investigate whether customer preferences have changed, competitors have become more attractive, or other factors are influencing sales. Exploratory analytics helps generate initial ideas and hypotheses rather than providing final answers.

### Descriptive Questions
These questions focus on understanding the current situation using factual data. Examples include identifying who the customers are, how much they spend, their share of wallet, and how customers can be segmented into different groups. Descriptive analytics relies on hard data to summarize and understand customer behavior.

### Causal Questions
These questions examine cause-and-effect relationships. For example, a company may want to know whether changing a website landing page will increase or decrease click-through rates. Answering causal questions requires carefully designed data collection and analysis methods.

## Exploratory Data Collection

Exploratory data collection is often the first step in analytics. Its goal is to develop initial insights, identify potential problems, and guide future, more rigorous analysis.

### Focus Groups
A focus group is a research method where a small group of customers discusses a brand, product, or service in an open and relatively unstructured environment. The objective is to uncover customer opinions, feelings, motivations, and pain points that may not be visible through numerical data alone.

### Online Customer Communities
Modern analytics has expanded focus groups into online customer communities. These communities involve larger groups of customers interacting over extended periods. They help organizations build stronger customer relationships, gather deeper insights, increase customer engagement, and obtain feedback more quickly. However, the return on investment (ROI) can be difficult to predict because valuable insights may or may not emerge from the community over time.

## Key Takeaway

Descriptive analytics is not just about collecting data; it is about using the right data to answer business questions and support decisions. Understanding whether a question is exploratory, descriptive, or causal helps determine what type of data should be collected and how it should be analyzed.
