# 1. Advertising Attribution Problem

## Business Question
Which advertising channel deserves credit for a customer's purchase?

Companies spend money on:

- TV advertising
- Radio advertising
- Banner advertisements
- Search engine marketing
- Social media marketing

The challenge is determining which channel actually influenced the purchase. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

### Traditional Approach: Last-Click Attribution

Most firms use **last-click attribution**.

Example:

Customer journey:

1. Sees a banner ad
2. Watches a TV advertisement
3. Searches on Google
4. Visits company website
5. Purchases product

Last-click attribution gives 100% credit to Google because it was the final interaction before purchase. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

### Problem

The earlier touchpoints may have influenced the customer's decision.

Analytics attempts to evaluate the entire customer journey rather than only the final click. This provides a more accurate estimate of advertising ROI. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

# 2. Multi-Channel Cannibalization

## Business Question

If a company launches a new channel, will it reduce performance of existing channels?

Example:

A media company launches:

- TV platform
- Website
- Streaming platform
- Mobile application

Management worries:

- Will mobile reduce TV viewing?
- Will mobile reduce website traffic?
- Will mobile reduce streaming usage?

This phenomenon is called **channel cannibalization**. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

### ESPN World Cup Example

Researchers studied whether mobile viewing reduced other forms of sports consumption.

Results:

- Mobile usage did cannibalize some TV viewing.
- Mobile usage did cannibalize some web viewing.
- However overall customer engagement increased.

The company gained an additional channel to interact with customers. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

### Key Lesson

Businesses should optimize:

**Total customer engagement**

instead of

**Individual channel performance**

Analytics helps measure this effect. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

# 3. Value of Discounts and Promotions

## Business Question

How valuable is a discount coupon?

Traditional measurement:

- Send discount coupon
- Observe whether customer buys more

This measures only direct impact. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

### Hidden Impact

Suppose:

- Eric receives a golf discount
- Eric decides to play golf
- Eric invites 3 friends

Now:

- Eric generated revenue
- His friends generated additional revenue

The coupon influenced more than one customer. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

### Social Network Effect

Research found:

- About 2/3 of value comes directly from recipient
- About 1/3 comes from people influenced by recipient

Therefore promotion effectiveness is often underestimated if social influence is ignored. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

# 4. Word-of-Mouth Analytics

## Business Question

Which customers create the most value?

Not all customers are equally valuable.

Two customers may spend the same amount.

However:

- Customer A buys products
- Customer B buys products and recommends them to friends

Customer B creates greater total value. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

### Modern Analytics

Using:

- Social network data
- Referral data
- Communication data
- Social media interactions

Companies can estimate:

- Influence scores
- Referral impact
- Network effects

This helps identify highly influential customers. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

# 5. Facebook ROI Problem

## Business Question

Do Facebook likes actually increase sales?

Many companies assume:

More Likes → More Sales

But this is not enough. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

### Wrong Analysis

If you run:

Purchase = f(Facebook Likes)

you will probably find a positive relationship.

However this does not prove Facebook caused the purchase. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

### Better Analysis

Include:

- TV advertising
- Online advertising
- Search advertising
- Facebook activity

Then test whether Facebook still adds predictive power.

If Facebook remains significant, it provides unique information that other channels do not. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}

# 6. Short-Term vs Long-Term Advertising Effects

Analytics shows different channels affect customers differently.

### Facebook and Online Ads

Characteristics:

- Immediate impact
- Short-lived influence
- Customers forget quickly

### Television Advertising

Characteristics:

- Slower effect
- Longer-lasting influence
- Stronger brand-building effect

Analytics helps measure both short-term and long-term effects separately. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}

# 7. Market Efficiency Finding

One surprising finding from analytics research:

More expensive advertising channels often create proportionally larger impact.

Example:

- TV ad costs 1000× more than Facebook ad.
- TV ad may generate approximately 1000× more economic value.

This suggests advertising markets are relatively efficient, meaning prices roughly reflect effectiveness. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}
