# Operations Analytics - Week 4, Session 4: Solving the Newsvendor Problem with Simulation and Optimization

This session revisits the classic **Newsvendor Problem** introduced earlier in the course and demonstrates how **simulation** and **optimization** can be combined to determine the best inventory level under uncertain demand. The objective is to find an order quantity that maximizes expected profit while also considering risk.

The business scenario involves a retailer selling products called **wodgets**. The key information is:

* Selling Price = 12 talers per unit
* Unit Cost = 3 talers per unit
* Salvage Value = 0
* Demand is uncertain

Historical demand analysis suggests that future demand follows a **Normal Distribution** with:

* Mean Demand = 52.81 units
* Standard Deviation = 15.1 units

Because future demand is uncertain, simulation is used to generate possible demand scenarios and evaluate different order quantities.

### Building the Simulation Model

Let:

* (Q) = Order Quantity
* (D) = Demand

Since sales cannot exceed either demand or available inventory:

[
Sales = \min(D,Q)
]

Revenue is:

[
12 \times \min(D,Q)
]

Cost is:

[
3 \times Q
]

Profit becomes:

[
Profit = 12 \times \min(D,Q) - 3Q
]

For a chosen order quantity, multiple demand values are generated from the demand distribution. Profit is calculated for each simulated demand value, creating a sample of possible profit outcomes.

### Estimating Reward

The **average profit** across all simulation runs is used as an estimate of expected profit. This becomes the objective function that the business wants to maximize.

Using 50 simulated demand samples, Excel Solver is used to choose the order quantity that maximizes average profit. The optimization problem is:

* Objective: Maximize Average Profit
* Decision Variable: Order Quantity (Q)
* Constraint: (Q \geq 0)

Because the profit formula contains the MIN function, the model is nonlinear and is solved using Excel's **GRG Nonlinear Solver**.

### Profit-Maximizing Solution

After optimization:

* Optimal Order Quantity ≈ **59.5 units**
* Estimated Average Profit ≈ **402 talers**

This order quantity provides the highest estimated expected profit based on the simulated demand sample.

### Introducing Risk

While maximizing profit is important, managers often care about risk as well. In this session, risk is measured using the **standard deviation of profit**.

* Higher Standard Deviation = Higher Risk
* Lower Standard Deviation = More Stable Profits

For the profit-maximizing solution:

* Average Profit ≈ 402 talers
* Standard Deviation of Profit ≈ 158 talers

This solution offers high reward but also relatively high risk.

### Risk-Constrained Optimization

Suppose management decides that the standard deviation of profit should not exceed **125 talers**.

A new optimization constraint is added:

[
Standard\ Deviation \leq 125
]

The optimization is solved again with this risk limit. The new solution becomes:

* Optimal Order Quantity ≈ **50.3 units**
* Average Profit ≈ **382 talers**
* Standard Deviation ≈ **125 talers**

Reducing the order quantity lowers profit variability and satisfies the risk requirement. However, the expected profit also decreases.

### Risk–Reward Trade-Off

The most important lesson is that reducing risk usually comes at the cost of reducing reward.

| Scenario                  | Order Quantity | Average Profit | Risk (Std Dev) |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| Profit Maximization       | 59.5           | 402            | 158            |
| Risk-Constrained Solution | 50.3           | 382            | 125            |

The company sacrifices some expected profit in exchange for more predictable outcomes.

### Key Takeaways

The Newsvendor Problem demonstrates how businesses can make inventory decisions under uncertainty using analytics. Simulation generates realistic demand scenarios, optimization identifies the order quantity that maximizes expected profit, and risk constraints allow managers to balance profitability and uncertainty. Together, **simulation, optimization, and risk analysis** provide a practical framework for making better operational decisions when future demand is unknown.
