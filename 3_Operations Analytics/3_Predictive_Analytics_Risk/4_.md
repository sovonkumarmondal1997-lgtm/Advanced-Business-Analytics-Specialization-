# Operations Analytics - Week 3 Review Session: Simulation Modeling with the Europa Solvent Example

This review session reinforces the simulation concepts introduced in Week 3 through a practical business example involving **Europa Solvent Industries (ESI)**. The company produces a chemical solvent and must deliver **35 tons per day** under a contract that pays **€10,000 per ton**. However, actual daily production is uncertain and can vary around the target output. Historical data indicates that daily production follows a **Normal Distribution** with:

* Mean = 35 tons
* Standard Deviation = 7 tons

The production cost is **€9,500 per ton**. If production exceeds 35 tons, the excess must be sold at a discounted salvage price of **€9,000 per ton**. If production falls below 35 tons, the company incurs a **€2,000 penalty for each missing ton**. Because production is uncertain, daily profit is also uncertain and must be analyzed using simulation.

To understand the profit structure, the session first examines two examples.

When production equals **31 tons**, all output is sold at the contract price. Revenue equals €310,000, production cost equals €294,500, and a shortage penalty of €8,000 is incurred because production is 4 tons below the target. The resulting profit is:

[
310,000 - 294,500 - 8,000 = 7,500
]

Thus, daily profit is **€7,500**.

When production equals **37 tons**, the first 35 tons are sold at €10,000 per ton, while the extra 2 tons are sold at the discounted salvage price of €9,000 per ton. Revenue equals €368,000, production cost equals €351,500, resulting in a profit of:

[
368,000 - 351,500 = 16,500
]

Thus, daily profit is **€16,500**.

Using these two situations, a piecewise profit model is developed.

### Case 1: Production Below 35 Tons

[
Profit = 2500P - 70,000
]

### Case 2: Production At or Above 35 Tons

[
Profit = -500P + 35,000
]

These two formulas can be combined in Excel using an **IF function**, allowing profit to be automatically calculated for any production value.

The session then demonstrates how to perform a simulation using **100 simulation runs** and a random seed of **123**. Excel's **Analysis ToolPak** is used to generate 100 random production values from a Normal distribution with mean 35 and standard deviation 7. Each simulated production value is passed through the profit formula to calculate a corresponding daily profit. This creates a sample of possible future profit outcomes.

Once the simulation is complete, the resulting profit sample is analyzed using descriptive statistics.

### Reward Measure

The **sample mean** of the simulated profit values estimates the expected daily profit.

### Risk Measure

The **sample standard deviation** of the simulated profit values measures profit variability and uncertainty.

For the simulation performed in the session:

* **Estimated Expected Daily Profit ≈ €8,752**
* **Estimated Standard Deviation of Daily Profit ≈ €9,415**

These estimates represent the reward and risk associated with the company's production process.

The key lesson is that simulation converts uncertain production outcomes into a distribution of possible profit outcomes. By generating many future scenarios and analyzing the resulting profit values, managers can estimate both expected performance and risk, allowing better decision-making in uncertain operating environments.
